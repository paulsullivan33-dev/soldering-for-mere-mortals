# Safety

Short chapter. Read all of it. Low-voltage electronics is a safe
hobby *because* people respect the few things that bite.

## Electricity

- **Mains (wall outlet) is off-limits.** 120V/240V AC can kill you,
  and mistakes happen in milliseconds. Never open mains-powered
  equipment, never wire a mains plug, never "just" probe a live
  outlet as a beginner. This guide's circuits are all low-voltage
  DC for a reason.
- **Low voltage is forgiving, not harmless.** A car battery or a
  big LiPo can source *hundreds* of amps into a short — molten
  wire, fire. Respect high-current sources even at 12V.
- **Capacitors hold charge.** Big electrolytics (power supplies,
  especially old CRTs/microwaves — don't open those, ever) can
  bite after unplugging. Discharge through a resistor, not a
  screwdriver (the spark welds).

## Heat and fumes

- **The iron is 350°C.** It burns on contact, instantly. Always
  return it to the stand — never lay it on the bench. Assume every
  metal object near the work area is hot until proven otherwise.
- **Solder fumes:** the visible wisp is mostly flux vapor — an
  irritant, not nerve gas, but don't breathe it deliberately. A
  small fan blowing across the work (not at your face) is enough.
- **Lead solder:** wash your hands after soldering, don't eat at
  the bench, keep it from kids and pets. The danger is ingestion
  over time, not touching it. Lead-free exists if you prefer.

## Eyes and hands

- **Safety glasses** when clipping leads — cut wire ends fly
  fast and far. Regular glasses count.
- **Sharp things:** component leads, craft knives for cutting
  traces, fiberglass PCB edges. Cut away from yourself; the knife
  *will* slip eventually.
- **Tweezers, not fingers**, for holding small parts near the iron.

## Batteries

- **Lithium (LiPo/Li-ion):** puncture, crush, or overcharge = fire
  that's hard to put out. Charge on a non-flammable surface, never
  unattended; stop using puffy/damaged cells. Use a proper charger
  — not a random 5V supply.
- **Don't short batteries.** A paperclip across a 9V gets hot
  enough to burn in seconds. Cap or tape terminals in the parts
  bin.

## The mindset

None of this is meant to scare you off — millions solder safely.
It's meant to make the precautions *habits*: glasses on, fan on,
iron in the stand, hands washed. Five seconds of setup buys the
whole hobby.
