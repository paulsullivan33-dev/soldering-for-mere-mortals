# Soldering & Basic Electronics for Mere Mortals

Software stops at the screen. **Electronics** is what happens when
you want to touch the physical world — and **soldering** is how you
join the pieces permanently. This guide teaches both: enough theory
to not break things, and enough technique to make joints you're
proud of.

No CS degree required. No electronics background assumed. Ideas
first, then a hot iron in your hand.

## What this guide covers

- **What electronics is** — voltage, current, and resistance
- **Ohm's law** — the one equation that runs everything
- **Components** — resistors, capacitors, diodes, LEDs, transistors:
  what each one *does*
- **Reading resistors** — the color code, decoded
- **The LED circuit** — your first real calculation, end to end
- **Soldering** — tools, technique, and the perfect joint
- **Reading your joints** — good vs. cold vs. bridged
- **Breadboard to permanent** — perfboard and when to commit
- **The multimeter** — measuring voltage, current, resistance, and
  continuity
- **Power supplies** — batteries, wall warts, regulators, polarity
- **Safety** — the chapter that matters most
- **Troubleshooting** — it doesn't work: the checklist

## What it doesn't cover

Mains (wall-outlet) wiring — **never** open or modify mains-powered
equipment as a beginner; that's electrician territory. RF design,
PCB layout software, or surface-mount rework at scale. This guide is
about low-voltage DC electronics and through-hole soldering: the
on-ramp, done right.

## How to use this guide

Every calculation was verified before shipping — the numbers are
real. **Check:** blocks confirm your math. **Careful:** marks
anything involving heat, sharp things, or electricity that bites.
The [Safety](safety.md) chapter is short; read it first if you're
impatient.

A basic soldering iron, some solder, and a $15 component kit are all
you need to follow along hands-on.
